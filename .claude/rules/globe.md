# The Vienna globe

Loads when you touch the Vienna panel, the globe or its data. Sibling rules: `card.md`, `motion.md`, `sky.md`.

- Vienna easter egg: the Vienna topic opens `city-panel.tsx` (`popover="auto"`; on screens at least 40rem wide and 34rem tall `src/lib/anchored-popovers.ts` places it with `@floating-ui/dom` (flip, shift, size, like Radix) next to the word, kept clear of the safe areas; a bottom sheet on phones and short landscape screens, inside the safe areas; every topic panel that scrolls shows a soft fade at its bottom, a scroll-driven `::after` that fades out at the end and is absent where scroll timelines are unsupported): the coat of arms (`vienna-arms.tsx`: gradient shield and cross, bevel, drop shadow, a glint every 7 s), a few facts about the city (`en.city.facts`, true and checkable only) and a "Show on the globe" button, in a compact layout (1rem title, small arms, a left-aligned 2.25rem button). That button is the only island: `city-globe-launcher.tsx` hides the panel, opens a `<dialog>` modal through a portal (the panel heading on top, the globe filling the rest; up to 64rem by 46rem, full screen below 40rem; the dialog pads the windowed sheet by the four `env(safe-area-inset-*)` values, as the page uses `viewport-fit=cover`, and the full-screen sheet pads itself; Escape, the close button or the scrim close it and focus goes back to "Vienna"), and only then imports `city-globe-stage.tsx` with `src/lib/city-globe.ts` and three.js. A progress bar (teal to violet, with a sheen and a percentage) creeps while the chunk downloads and jumps at real milestones: code loaded, globe built, shaders compiled and the first frame drawn (`onReady`). A failed load says so with "Try again", then "Reload the page" (a stale chunk after a deploy needs a reload).
- Globe look: a quiet night-side style in deep navy and slate (`globePalette` in `theme.ts`), opened on Vienna: a short eased spin and zoom from 1.3x end with Vienna in the centre at 3.2x (`homeZoom`); "Back to Vienna", `0` and Home return there, and users can zoom out to 1x. The camera looks straight down up to 1.3x and tilts toward the south as it zooms in (up to 26° at 3.4x), so Vienna's light pillar stands out. Land, Austria and a hairline shore come from a land mask (world 2048x1024, rings unwrapped at the 180° meridian; a 32 px per degree Europe mask fades in past 1.4x zoom, up to 9x). Day and night follow the real sun: `subsolarPoint` (`src/lib/sun.ts`, tested against the 2026 equinox, both solstices and the equation of time) turns the current UTC time into the point under the sun, refreshed every minute, so every visitor sees the same globe. The night half is dark (land and Austria only faintly visible), a faint dusk band marks the terminator, the daylit half gets a very soft cool sheen, and a thin inner rim and a faint outer halo light only the sunlit edge.
- Globe marks: Vienna is a gold beacon (a 60 px point: a bright core, a soft glow and one strong ring every 3.4 s, drawn last without the depth test, so the tilted globe never cuts it) with a thin light pillar (an additive camera-facing strip along the surface normal, 0.36 radii tall, fading upward, a faint pulse running up it every 2.8 s; it fades out when seen end-on or from behind the globe) and a larger gold label led by the round `circle-flags:at` (rendered by `city-globe-stage.tsx`, placed by the globe); the 100 largest metro areas (urban agglomerations), at most three per country, are small quiet pale dots without pulses: `src/lib/world-cities.ts` holds them as typed data (English name, country, population, latitude, longitude; sorted by population; from UN DESA World Urbanization Prospects 2018, File 12 (urban agglomerations), the 2025 projection, with ISO country codes in which Hong Kong and Taiwan count on their own and coordinates from Wikipedia; tested: 100 rows, at most three per country, valid coordinates, unique names, no Vienna, sorted). City names are data, like the product names; the modal credits the source in one muted line (`en.city.citiesSource`).
- Globe labels: labels are HTML text (`aria-hidden`) shown for every place that faces the viewer and sits in the view, from 30% opacity at the limb to full; `placeLabels` (`src/lib/globe-labels.ts`, tested) never drops one: it tries the right, left, top and bottom of each dot, then its four corners (Vienna first), scores a spot by its overlap with the labels already placed (closer than 3 px counts), with every dot, with how far it had to be clamped into the view and with the blocked areas: the zoom buttons and the credit line, the hint until the first interaction, and the pillar's screen strip (base to tip, 7 px each side, while the pillar shows). A blocked area within 24 px of a view edge reaches that edge, an overlap with one counts four times its area (its 3 px margin once), and a spot that touches one may also slide clear of it at the cost of a clamp. It keeps the spot a label had in the previous frame unless another one costs less than three quarters of it minus a 3 px strip of the label's height, and leaves overlaps only where no spot is free. When the intro settles the view snaps to its goal and, until the first interaction, every layout ignores the previous spots, so the opening view is the same at any frame rate and under reduced motion. City labels are 10 px below `40rem` (Vienna's stays 14 px), which cuts the overlaps of the phone overview at 1x. The layout reruns only when the view or the size changes. The globe sizes itself from the canvas layout size (`clientWidth`, `clientHeight`): the dialog opens with a scale animation, so `getBoundingClientRect` measured it 2% small and pushed every label off its dot. Screen readers get one hidden list of the cities (`en.city.citiesLabel`, no count in the copy).
- Globe input: drag, wheel, pinch, buttons, arrow keys, `+`, `-`, `0`; until the first interaction a hint names the gestures (`en.city.hint`, or `en.city.hintTouch` without "Scroll" on coarse pointers, swapped by CSS). A fresh canvas and context per open, none while closed. While the modal is open `holdScene` (`src/lib/scene-hold.ts`) pauses the sky and planets, so one context draws; the globe keeps drawing for the beacon ring and the pillar pulse (about 30 fps when idle, nothing while the tab is hidden) and, under reduced motion or the pause, shows a still ring and pillar, skips the opening spin, draws on demand and stops when nothing moves. A software renderer gets a short message instead of the globe.
