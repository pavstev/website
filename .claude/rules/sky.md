# Sky, planets and shaders

Loads when you touch the sky, the planets, the logo planets or any WebGL helper. Sibling rules: `card.md`, `motion.md`, `globe.md`.

- Logo planets: `product-mark.tsx` renders `span.logo-planet[data-logo]` with an `aria-hidden` inline `svg` (a disc `circle`, then a `g[transform]` of `path[data-ink="mark"|"accent"]`) filled from the inline `--logo-disc`, `--logo-mark` and `--logo-accent`. That flat mark is the fallback (no JS, no WebGL, software renderer, Save-Data, context loss, forced colors, print) and the source the canvas reads, so the path data ships once. `RepoPlanetsCanvas` sits in `.card-strips` and uses its parent as the stage; `repo-planets.ts` finds `.repo-planet` and `.logo-planet` nodes and sets `data-planets="gl"` on the stage itself, and `@media screen and (forced-colors: none)` hides `[data-planets="gl"] .logo-planet svg` with a 300 ms fade once the first frame (the sunrise start pose) is drawn. `createLogoPlanet` (`src/lib/logo-planet.ts`) draws the paths with `Path2D` into a 256x256 mask (mark ink red, accent ink green, `lighter`; the `<g>` transform applied by hand, as `Path2D` ignores ancestor transforms), uploads it as a texture (no color conversion, anisotropy `min(4, max)`, mipmaps, the settings three.js used before the port) and draws it with one shared logo program: the planet lighting with the mask printed on the front hemisphere only, the logo faintly visible on the night side. A missing color or mask throws, and the whole strip falls back to CSS. Repo and logo planets use the fixed view vector `vec3(0, 0, 1)` (orthographic camera), so a rim does not depend on the tile position. Motion (`src/lib/logo-motion.ts`, pure, tested): a sunrise once per page view from -0.9 rad (turned away, at the terminator, so the mark is in view almost at once) to 0, ease-out cubic, 1.6 s, starting the first time the logo is 60% visible while the canvas runs (one `IntersectionObserver` per logo), and never before the first frame is drawn, so the first frame shows the start pose even while the shaders still compile; then a plus or minus 0.18 rad wobble (period about 9 s, never a full turn), small enough that the whole mark stays inside the disc. Hover or `:focus-visible` in the tile spins it up like the repo planets (speed x3.4, at most 0.28 rad, the `uHot` glow). A held or paused scene, a freeze or reduced motion snaps an unfinished sunrise to the rest pose with one draw; a hidden tab or an off-screen stage resumes and finishes it. Reduced motion draws one still frame at the rest pose.
- Sky (`space-background.tsx`, `data-space-state`: `running`, `paused`, `static`, `fallback`): one raw WebGL fragment shader (the GLSL lives in `src/lib/sky-shader.ts`, shared with the image generator) with the "Eclipse" look the owner tuned on 2026-10-12 in the claude.ai artifact "Eclipse Lab" (knob values baked in as shader constants; change the look there first, then copy the values). The canvas is `position: fixed; bottom: 0` with `height: 100lvh` (the large viewport, so a phone's collapsing toolbar never shows a gap), so the sky stays put while the page scrolls. Shader units are the canvas height (`u_view = canvas.height`) and the screen bottom is the horizon: the low clouds sit at the bottom of every screen, a phone at 390x844 included, at scroll 0. A scroll redraws (one frame through `requestAnimationFrame` while stopped), because the portrait and the bio move over the fixed sky; so does the end of a `#main-content` transition (`transitionend`, the privacy OK padding change that re-centers the card), so a paused or still sky moves the corona and the bio shield with it. The corona, pink flares, a thin rim and a diamond-ring flash (about every 7.7 s) are centered on `[data-card] [data-portrait]`, read with `getBoundingClientRect` every frame (so intro transforms, layout changes and scrolling follow) and never moved by the pointer; only the stars take a small pointer parallax on fine pointers. The corona streamers turn around the portrait (about one turn per 38 s), the fine rays turn slowly the other way and the flares drift with them. Low clouds: a stratocumulus bank in two layers up to about a third of the screen above its bottom. Each layer is rows of irregular cells (a jittered cell grid, warped by fBm, rows flatter and denser toward the horizon, more gaps higher up, a solid stratus band at the bottom) with fBm-frayed edges. Light is a two-sample march: the density a step below a point (toward the horizon glow) sets the underlight, the density a step above sets the sky light on the tops, and thin parts let the glow through. Thin high wisps sit between 0.26 and 0.8 of the screen. Cloud color: owner pick A2 "Moonlit slate" on 2026-10-12 (claude.ai artifact <https://claude.ai/artifact/Mu4abdVv4GVRfNSng8ZeEu>): `cloudTone` = `vec4(1, 0.55, 0.5, 0.8)` (undo the global hue turn, underglow gain, saturation, how much the glow fades by day) and `cloudGlow` = `vec3(1, 0.78, 0.58)`, a pale amber underlight on slate clouds. Change both in the pick page first. Stars grow denser toward the screen top (`starTop` 1.7 at the top, today's density below 0.3; the big star layer takes the least of it). Bio shield: the bio box (`[data-card] .bio`, read every frame like the portrait, passed as `u_quiet`) dims the stars behind it to 35% and rain and snow by 70%, and after tone mapping a luminance cap (`quietLuma` 0.034 linear, feathered over 0.06 of the screen) keeps the sky behind the bio dark enough for at least 6.9:1 bio contrast in every weather, day and night. Weather (owner choice 2026-10-12: live Vienna weather, below the clouds only weather, no skyline): `src/lib/weather.ts` (pure, tested) maps the `/weather` answer (see `docs/architecture.md`) to `SkyWeather`: `cover` (the larger of low, 0.85 x mid and 0.6 x total cover, at least 0.12), `overcast` (0.9 x a smoothstep of total cover from 55 to 100%, at least 0.85 with rain or snow), `high` (0.1 + 0.9 x high cover), `fog` (visibility under 10 km, at least 0.85 for WMO fog codes 45 and 48), `rain` and `snow` (from the WMO code and the amounts), `storm` (codes 95, 96, 99), `slant` (east wind / 60 km/h, at most 0.5) and `drift`. Uniforms: `u_weather` = cover, overcast, high, fog; `u_fall` = rain, snow, storm, slant. Cover thickens and raises the bank (up to 36% taller) and fills its gaps; overcast lays a flat gray deck over the sky (85%) and dims the stars by 92% and the horizon glow by half; high scales the wisps (0.5 = the old look); fog rolls up from the bottom; rain draws dark shafts under the bank and two layers of slanted streaks; snow draws three layers of drifting flakes; storm adds a lightning flash in about half of the slots of 9 cloud-time units (about 4.9 s at `speed` 1.82) that lights the clouds near a random spot. Rain, snow and the shaft texture run on `mod(tm, 2000)` (fine on `highp`); each rain layer hashes its rows modulo 240 or 380, which divide 2000 x its fall speed, so rain never jumps at the wrap, while snow and the shafts change once per wrap (about every 18 minutes). The `?weather=` presets drift left with rain slanted left (`slant` -0.12), like live wind. The browser asks `/weather` once at start and then on a 15 minute `setInterval`, whether the sky runs or not (production builds only; `pnpm dev` and `pnpm preview` have no Worker, and under `pnpm preview` the 404 shows as one console error, so test the route with `pnpm exec wrangler dev`, the `worker` entry in `.claude/launch.json`). The values ease in over about 3 s while the sky runs; any stop (pause, hold, hidden tab, blur, freeze, reduced motion) snaps them to the target, and an answer that arrives while stopped is drawn at once. No answer, `null` or an error keeps the fallback (`fallbackSky`: cover 0.6, high 0.5, the pre-weather look); the Worker never answers 5xx, so live there is no console output. `?weather=clear|partly|overcast|rain|snow|fog|storm` forces a preset (`weatherPresets`) and skips the fetch, for tests. The image generator draws the fallback with no puffs. Wind: `drift` is the real east-west wind (`-sign(east) x clamp(|east| / 15 km/h, 0.5, 2)`, 0.5 under 1 km/h), so the clouds move with the wind, east to the right; the fallback drifts left at 1x. Cloud motion uses two clocks: `u_cloudTime` only morphs the clouds slowly; `u_cloudShift` moves them. Every cloud feature, the cell warp and the bank height are read at `x + shift`, so the clouds move as rigid layers and never warp, shear or mix. Swipe gust: mouse, pen and touch movement in the lowest 0.8 of the screen adds gust energy (1.8 per screen height of travel, at most 1; on touch the up-down part counts a quarter, so a scroll gives a small push and a sideways drag the full one; touch is read from `touchmove`, because the browser cancels the pointer once it starts a scroll; a lifted finger starts fresh); the energy eases back over about 2.3 s, and the shift clock runs `drift - 2.5 x smoothstep(energy) x dir` times normal, so a swipe adds up to 2.5 times the base speed in the swipe's horizontal direction (3.5x at the fallback drift, up to 4.5x with a strong wind); a new gust starts in the wind's direction, so a straight up or down swipe speeds the clouds the way they already move. Swipe puffs (owner pick B1 "Swell", `puffMode` 0): the same movement in the lowest 0.55 of the screen drops a puff every 0.11 screen heights along the path (10 slots in `u_puffs`, a new one replaces the weakest; at most 0.3 above the bottom). A puff rises over 0.7 s to 0.9 and settles over about 4 s; it rides with its cloud layer and swells the bank around it (more cells, a taller top). It only adds cloud: a proof render showed 0 pixels losing cloud and at most 3/255 change outside the puff. `puffMode` 1 ("Condense", fresh cloud appears in clear sky) is the other pick. `u_puffOn` skips the puff loop when no puff is visible. The pointer never moves cloud shapes, and the far layer takes no pointer parallax. A stopped sky ignores pointer and touch movement (no gust, no puffs), and a hold, a pause or a hidden tab clears the gust and the puffs; the sky keeps full frame rate while the energy is above 0.01 or a puff is visible. Daylight: the sky follows the Vienna clock (owner choice 2026-10-12), not the visitor's (`u_daylight` = day, twilight, dusk from `skyPhase` in `src/lib/sky-clock.ts`, tested: the Europe/Vienna wall time from `Intl.DateTimeFormat`, sunrise and sunset at latitude 48.21 with the summer-time shift read from that time, refreshed every 30 s). By day the sky lifts toward azure, stars and the corona fade, and the clouds get lighter; dawn glows rose and dusk amber. The owner tuned these values on 2026-10-12 in the claude.ai artifact "Sky Clock Lab"; change them there first. A shooting star every 2 to 14 s (1.45 s flight) starts above the screen center, hidden under overcast; any pointerdown sends a faint ripple. Pixel-sized details (star cores, rim, flash, snowflakes) are scaled by `u_labPx` to match the lab at 1.2 lab pixels per CSS pixel. It renders at 0.75x (then 0.5x, then 0.35x without clouds on slow frames), at most 1.4 M pixels, caps at about 90 fps, drops to 30 fps after 800 ms without input (a gust or a puff keeps it at full rate until it eases back), pauses when hidden or blurred, freezes after eight frames in a row over 100 ms, and draws one still frame under reduced motion and on a software renderer (`src/lib/webgl.ts`: SwiftShader, llvmpipe, Microsoft Basic Render). Its `u_time` starts at 0 and wraps after a day of running time (1024 s without `highp`); it runs on an accumulated clock (`createFrameClock` in `src/lib/frame-clock.ts`, tested: the sum of frame steps, each capped at 100 ms and never negative; `pause()` on every stop and right before every draw made while stopped, such as resize and scroll frames), so after a pause, a hold or a hidden tab it resumes where it stopped. The shooting star uses its own progress value. There is no separate cloud canvas and no `sky:pulse` event since 2026-10-12.
- No renderer blocks on shader compiles: the planets link their programs with `KHR_parallel_shader_compile` and poll before the first draw (`whenLinked` in `planet-gl.ts`, link errors checked only outside production); the globe, the only three.js user (a lazy chunk loaded on its click), compiles with `renderer.compileAsync` and sets `renderer.debug.checkShaderErrors` only outside production. The planet port (2026-10-12) kept every GLSL body, the geometry, the sRGB-to-linear uniform colors, the draw states and order, and took about 104 KB brotli off the first visit (0 pixel diff on the still frame). Measured with `~/powertools/scripts/site-probe.mjs`: no blocking GL calls, startup long tasks fell from 955 ms to 67 ms.
