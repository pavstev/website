# Sky, planets and shaders

Loads when you touch the sky, the planets, the logo planets or any WebGL helper. Sibling rules: `card.md`, `motion.md`, `globe.md`.

- Logo planets: `product-mark.tsx` renders `span.logo-planet[data-logo]` with an `aria-hidden` inline `svg` (a disc `circle`, then a `g[transform]` of `path[data-ink="mark"|"accent"]`) filled from the inline `--logo-disc`, `--logo-mark` and `--logo-accent`. That flat mark is the fallback (no JS, no WebGL, software renderer, Save-Data, context loss, forced colors, print) and the source the canvas reads, so the path data ships once. `RepoPlanetsCanvas` sits in `.card-strips` and uses its parent as the stage; `repo-planets.ts` finds `.repo-planet` and `.logo-planet` nodes and sets `data-planets="gl"` on the stage itself, and `@media screen and (forced-colors: none)` hides `[data-planets="gl"] .logo-planet svg` with a 300 ms fade once the first frame (the sunrise start pose) is drawn. `createLogoPlanet` (`src/lib/logo-planet.ts`) draws the paths with `Path2D` into a 256x256 mask (mark ink red, accent ink green, `lighter`; the `<g>` transform applied by hand, as `Path2D` ignores ancestor transforms), uploads it as a texture (no color conversion, anisotropy `min(4, max)`, mipmaps, the settings three.js used before the port) and draws it with one shared logo program: the planet lighting with the mask printed on the front hemisphere only, the logo faintly visible on the night side. A missing color or mask throws, and the whole strip falls back to CSS. Repo and logo planets use the fixed view vector `vec3(0, 0, 1)` (orthographic camera), so a rim does not depend on the tile position. Motion (`src/lib/logo-motion.ts`, pure, tested): a sunrise once per page view from -0.9 rad (turned away, at the terminator, so the mark is in view almost at once) to 0, ease-out cubic, 1.6 s, starting the first time the logo is 60% visible while the canvas runs (one `IntersectionObserver` per logo), and never before the first frame is drawn, so the first frame shows the start pose even while the shaders still compile; then a plus or minus 0.18 rad wobble (period about 9 s, never a full turn), small enough that the whole mark stays inside the disc. Hover or `:focus-visible` in the tile spins it up like the repo planets (speed x3.4, at most 0.28 rad, the `uHot` glow). A held or paused scene, a freeze or reduced motion snaps an unfinished sunrise to the rest pose with one draw; a hidden tab or an off-screen stage resumes and finishes it. Reduced motion draws one still frame at the rest pose.
- Sky (`space-background.tsx`, `data-space-state`: `running`, `paused`, `static`, `fallback`): one raw WebGL fragment shader (the GLSL lives in `src/lib/sky-shader.ts`, shared with the image generator) with the "Eclipse" look the owner tuned on 2026-10-12 in the claude.ai artifact "Eclipse Lab" (knob values baked in as shader constants; change the look there first, then copy the values). The canvas is `position: absolute; inset: 0` inside `body` (`position: relative`, `display: flow-root`, so the dock's bottom margin stays inside and the canvas covers the whole page). It scrolls with the page, so the corona never lags behind the portrait and a scroll needs no redraw. Shader units are the viewport height (`u_view`) and the page bottom is the horizon: on pages that scroll, the horizon glow and the low clouds sit at the end of the page. The corona, pink flares, a thin rim and a diamond-ring flash (about every 7.7 s) are centered on `[data-card] [data-portrait]`, read with `getBoundingClientRect` every frame (so intro transforms and layout changes follow) and never moved by the pointer; only the stars take a small pointer parallax on fine pointers. Low clouds: two domain-warped fBm layers up to about a third of a viewport above the page bottom (banks, gaps and towers from a slow `variety` noise along the horizon), lit orange from below, with lifted bodies and silver edges so they stay visible on a bright laptop screen; on 2026-10-12 the owner asked for thicker, more distinct clouds (cover 0.6, opacity 0.95, sharper density edge, stronger billows), and each layer now reads its noise once more `shapeStep` lower, so the side facing the horizon glow is lit and the other side shaded (`face`), which gives the puffs volume; the bio still keeps 6.9:1 at night; plus thin high wisps between 0.26 and 0.8 viewport; they drift on their own clock (`u_cloudTime`). Cloud wind (2026-10-12): the sky asks for a WebGL2 context first (the GLSL 100 sky shader runs there unchanged) and falls back to WebGL1. On fine pointers with WebGL2 and a float color buffer, the pointer is a hand moving through air (`src/lib/cloud-wind.ts`): a 2D stable-fluids solver (Stam 1999: semi-Lagrangian advection, vorticity confinement after Fedkiw 2001, a 20-step Jacobi pressure projection, so the air stays incompressible) runs on a 48-row grid over the lowest 0.62 viewport of the page, with the ground as a wall and open sides and top. The pointer drags the air near its path toward its own velocity (a 0.075 viewport Gaussian along the segment, at most 3 viewports per second), so a swipe leaves a wake with eddies and a resting pointer is an obstacle. The clouds are passive tracers: a displacement field is advected with the same flow (the cloud noise is read where the air came from, `u_flow`) and drifts home over about 4.5 s, and fast air thins them (entrainment, at most 85%, heals over about 2 s). The near layer takes the full flow, the far layer 60%. Its programs link without blocking (`KHR_parallel_shader_compile`). A hold, a pause or a hidden tab clears the fields; 16 s after the last move it clears them and stops, so the rest frame is the plain shader (the image generator binds no flow texture, which reads as zero). Touch, WebGL1, reduced motion and software renderers get no wind. The corona streamers turn around the portrait (about one turn per 38 s), the fine rays turn slowly the other way and the flares drift with them. Daylight: the sky follows the visitor's local clock (`u_daylight` = day, twilight, dusk from `skyPhase` in `src/lib/sky-clock.ts`, tested; sunrise and sunset from the date at latitude 47 with the summer-time shift, refreshed every 30 s). By day the sky lifts toward azure, stars and the corona fade, and the clouds get lighter; dawn glows rose and dusk amber. The owner tuned these values on 2026-10-12 in the claude.ai artifact "Sky Clock Lab"; change them there first. The bio text keeps at least 6.9:1 against the sky at every hour (measured at 1440x900). A shooting star every 2 to 14 s (1.45 s flight) starts above the current viewport center; any pointerdown sends a faint ripple. Pixel-sized details (star cores, rim, flash) are scaled by `u_labPx` to match the lab at 1.2 lab pixels per CSS pixel. It renders at 0.75x (then 0.5x, then 0.35x without clouds on slow frames), at most 1.4 M pixels, caps at about 90 fps, drops to 30 fps after 800 ms without input (the cloud wind keeps it at full rate for 3 s after the last pointer move in the cloud zone), pauses when hidden or blurred, freezes after eight frames in a row over 100 ms, and draws one still frame under reduced motion and on a software renderer (`src/lib/webgl.ts`: SwiftShader, llvmpipe, Microsoft Basic Render). Its `u_time` starts at 0 and wraps after a day of running time (1024 s without `highp`); it runs on an accumulated clock (`createFrameClock` in `src/lib/frame-clock.ts`, tested: the sum of frame steps, each capped at 100 ms and never negative; `pause()` on every stop and right before every draw made while stopped, such as resize frames), so after a pause, a hold or a hidden tab it resumes where it stopped. The shooting star uses its own progress value. There is no separate cloud canvas and no `sky:pulse` event since 2026-10-12.
- No renderer blocks on shader compiles: the planets link their programs with `KHR_parallel_shader_compile` and poll before the first draw (`whenLinked` in `planet-gl.ts`, link errors checked only outside production); the globe, the only three.js user (a lazy chunk loaded on its click), compiles with `renderer.compileAsync` and sets `renderer.debug.checkShaderErrors` only outside production. The planet port (2026-10-12) kept every GLSL body, the geometry, the sRGB-to-linear uniform colors, the draw states and order, and took about 104 KB brotli off the first visit (0 pixel diff on the still frame). Measured with `~/powertools/scripts/site-probe.mjs`: no blocking GL calls, startup long tasks fell from 955 ms to 67 ms.
