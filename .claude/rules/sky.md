# Sky, planets and shaders

Loads when you touch the sky, the planets, the logo planets or any WebGL helper. Sibling rules: `card.md`, `motion.md`, `globe.md`.

- Logo planets: `product-mark.tsx` renders `span.logo-planet[data-logo]` with an `aria-hidden` inline `svg` (a disc `circle`, then a `g[transform]` of `path[data-ink="mark"|"accent"]`) filled from the inline `--logo-disc`, `--logo-mark` and `--logo-accent`. That flat mark is the fallback (no JS, no WebGL, software renderer, Save-Data, context loss, forced colors, print) and the source the canvas reads, so the path data ships once. `RepoPlanetsCanvas` sits in `.card-strips` and uses its parent as the stage; `repo-planets.ts` finds `.repo-planet` and `.logo-planet` nodes and sets `data-planets="gl"` on the stage itself, and `@media screen and (forced-colors: none)` hides `[data-planets="gl"] .logo-planet svg` with a 300 ms fade once the first frame (the sunrise start pose) is drawn. `createLogoPlanet` (`src/lib/logo-planet.ts`) draws the paths with `Path2D` into a 256x256 mask (mark ink red, accent ink green, `lighter`; the `<g>` transform applied by hand, as `Path2D` ignores ancestor transforms), uploads it as a texture (no color conversion, anisotropy `min(4, max)`, mipmaps, the settings three.js used before the port) and draws it with one shared logo program: the planet lighting with the mask printed on the front hemisphere only, the logo faintly visible on the night side. A missing color or mask throws, and the whole strip falls back to CSS. Repo and logo planets use the fixed view vector `vec3(0, 0, 1)` (orthographic camera), so a rim does not depend on the tile position. Motion (`src/lib/logo-motion.ts`, pure, tested): a sunrise once per page view from -0.9 rad (turned away, at the terminator, so the mark is in view almost at once) to 0, ease-out cubic, 1.6 s, starting the first time the logo is 60% visible while the canvas runs (one `IntersectionObserver` per logo), and never before the first frame is drawn, so the first frame shows the start pose even while the shaders still compile; then a plus or minus 0.18 rad wobble (period about 9 s, never a full turn), small enough that the whole mark stays inside the disc. Hover or `:focus-visible` in the tile spins it up like the repo planets (speed x3.4, at most 0.28 rad, the `uHot` glow). A held or paused scene, a freeze or reduced motion snaps an unfinished sunrise to the rest pose with one draw; a hidden tab or an off-screen stage resumes and finishes it. Reduced motion draws one still frame at the rest pose.
- Sky (`space-background.tsx`, `data-space-state`: `running`, `paused`, `static`, `fallback`): one raw WebGL fragment shader (the GLSL lives in `src/lib/sky-shader.ts`, shared with the image generator) with the "Eclipse" look the owner tuned on 2026-10-12 in the claude.ai artifact "Eclipse Lab" (knob values baked in as shader constants; change the look there first, then copy the values). The canvas is `position: fixed; bottom: 0` with `height: 100lvh` (the large viewport, so a phone's collapsing toolbar never shows a gap), so the sky stays put while the page scrolls. Shader units are the canvas height (`u_view = canvas.height`) and the screen bottom is the horizon: the low clouds sit at the bottom of every screen, a phone at 390x844 included, at scroll 0. Without the corona layer (below) a scroll redraws (one frame through `requestAnimationFrame` while stopped, full frame rate while running), because the portrait moves over the fixed sky; with the layer live a scroll draws nothing, since the layer scrolls on the compositor and the sky canvas then has nothing tied to the page (one trailing frame 180 ms after the scroll ends updates the text probe below). The end of a `#main-content` transition (`transitionend`, the privacy OK padding change that re-centers the card) also redraws, so a paused or still sky moves the corona with the card. The corona, pink flares, a thin rim and a diamond-ring flash (about every 7.7 s) are centered on the first `[data-eclipse]` element (the home portrait button or the index portrait; the post header has none, see Sky modes), read with `getBoundingClientRect` every frame (so intro transforms, layout changes and scrolling follow) and never moved by the pointer; only the stars take a small pointer parallax on fine pointers. A touch move only raises the frame rate through the gust or a puff it adds. The corona streamers turn around the portrait (about one turn per 38 s), the fine rays turn slowly the other way and the flares drift with them. Low clouds: a stratocumulus bank in two layers up to about a third of the screen above its bottom. Each layer is rows of irregular cells (a jittered cell grid, warped by fBm, rows flatter and denser toward the horizon, more gaps higher up, a solid stratus band at the bottom) with fBm-frayed edges. Light is a two-sample march: the density a step below a point (toward the horizon glow) sets the underlight, the density a step above sets the sky light on the tops, and thin parts let the glow through. Thin high wisps sit between 0.26 and 0.8 of the screen. Cloud color: owner pick A2 "Moonlit slate" on 2026-10-12 (claude.ai artifact <https://claude.ai/artifact/Mu4abdVv4GVRfNSng8ZeEu>): `cloudTone` = `vec4(1, 0.55, 0.5, 0.8)` (undo the global hue turn, underglow gain, saturation, how much the glow fades by day) and `cloudGlow` = `vec3(1, 0.78, 0.58)`, a pale amber underlight on slate clouds. Change both in the pick page first. Since C1 (below) the undersides also follow the sky's under-light (`underLight`: the city glow at night, the dawn or dusk band, a faint daylight), mixed in at `cloudFollow` 0.7; the A2 body colors stay. Stars grow denser toward the screen top (`starTop` 1.7 at the top, today's density below 0.3; the big star layer takes the least of it). Text over the sky (owner calls 2026-10-13: no dark boxes and no shadows behind text, and the text adapts to the sky): there are no shade boxes, no quiet zone, no `text-halo` and no footer shadow. Six texts take the `text-sky` utility (`globals.css`): the name, the bio, the two strip headings, the copyright and the weather credit (`inkTargets` in `src/lib/sky-ink.ts`, pure and tested, with the base color from `inkPalette` in `theme.ts` and the target: name 3, bio 6.9, the rest 4.5). `text-sky` is `color-mix(in srgb, var(--ink), white calc(var(--lift) * 100%))`, where `--ink` is the element's own color (`--foreground`, or `--foreground-soft` and `--muted-foreground` set on `.bio`, `.repo-heading`, `.card-footer` and `.weather-credit`) and `--lift` (a registered `<number>`, inherited, 0 to 1) eases over 0.8 s; the bio's topic words mix `--foreground` with the same `--lift`. The sky measures what is behind each text: `createSkyProbe` (`src/lib/sky-probe.ts`) draws the sky shader into a small framebuffer, 24x6 samples per text box (the text's own extent from a `Range`, clipped to the screen), through `u_probe` (origin and step in sky pixels; `u_probe.z` 0 means the normal draw), with the corona in (`u_split` 0), and reads it back with one `readPixels` at the start of the next frame (or 34 ms later while the sky is stopped), so the read never waits on the GPU. `skyLevel` takes the 95th percentile luminance plus 0.02 for the `body::before` tints, `liftFor` picks the smallest lift in steps of 0.05 that reaches the target times 1.08 (white if none does), and `nextLift` raises at once but lowers only past 0.12, so it never flickers. The probe is drawn right before a frame's draw, at most once a second, never in the first second (no extra stall at start) and never within 180 ms of a scroll; a stopped sky (paused, reduced motion, software renderer) wakes itself with one still frame when a probe is due, after start and after a scroll. The sky leaves `--lift` alone unless it changes. Browsers without `@property` read `var(--lift, 0)` and get the plain color. No JS, the fallback and print keep `--lift` 0 (print forces it). Dark ink was tried and dropped: the day clouds are patchy (95th percentile up to 0.35, 5th down to 0.05), so no single text color reads on them. Day clouds instead roll off: after tone mapping, by day (weight `smoothstep(0, 0.3, day)`, none at night, so stars keep their sparkle) and outside 1.4 to 2.4 portrait radii (the corona keeps its rim), a pixel's linear luminance above `dayKnee` 0.09 eases toward `dayCeiling` 0.15 (`knee + room x (1 - exp((knee - lum) / room))`, true sRGB curve, `srgbToLinear` and `linearToSrgb`), so white text keeps 4.5:1 over the brightest clouds. Measure with `.agents/handoffs/sky-tools/ink-contrast.mjs` (ring metric: the sky within 1.5 CSS px of the glyphs, 98th percentile, all six texts, three sizes, three times, seven weathers, phone at three scroll spots) under `wrangler dev`. Weather (owner choice 2026-10-12: live Vienna weather, below the clouds only weather, no skyline): `src/lib/weather.ts` (pure, tested) maps the `/weather` answer (see `docs/architecture.md`) to `SkyWeather`: `cover` (the larger of low, 0.85 x mid and 0.6 x total cover, at least 0.12), `overcast` (0.9 x a smoothstep of total cover from 55 to 100%, at least 0.85 with rain or snow), `high` (0.1 + 0.9 x high cover), `fog` (visibility under 10 km, at least 0.85 for WMO fog codes 45 and 48), `rain` and `snow` (from the WMO code and the amounts), `storm` (codes 95, 96, 99), `slant` (east wind / 60 km/h, at most 0.5) and `drift`. Uniforms: `u_weather` = cover, overcast, high, fog; `u_fall` = rain, snow, storm, slant. Cover thickens and raises the bank (up to 36% taller) and fills its gaps; overcast lays a gray deck over the sky (85%) with the CIE overcast sky brightness (1 + 2 sin h) / 3 under a cloud transmission 1 / (1 + 0.1125 tau), tau = 12 + 22 rain + 30 storm, lifted by snow on the ground, and dims the stars by 92%; high scales the wisps (0.5 = the old look); fog rolls up from the bottom; rain draws dark shafts under the bank and two layers of slanted streaks; snow draws three layers of drifting flakes (both at `precip` 0.5: dimmer streaks, fewer flakes); storm adds a lightning flash in about half of the slots of 9 cloud-time units (about 4.9 s at `speed` 1.82) that lights the clouds near a random spot. Rain, snow and the shaft texture run on `mod(tm, 2000)` (fine on `highp`); each rain layer hashes its rows modulo 240 or 380, which divide 2000 x its fall speed, so rain never jumps at the wrap, while snow and the shafts change once per wrap (about every 18 minutes). The `?weather=` presets drift left with rain slanted left (`slant` -0.12), like live wind. The browser asks `/weather` once at start and then on a 15 minute `setInterval`, whether the sky runs or not (production builds only; `pnpm dev` and `pnpm preview` have no Worker, and under `pnpm preview` the 404 shows as one console error, so test the route with `pnpm exec wrangler dev`, the `worker` entry in `.claude/launch.json`). The values ease in over about 3 s while the sky runs; any stop (pause, hold, hidden tab, blur, freeze, reduced motion) snaps them to the target, and an answer that arrives while stopped is drawn at once. No answer, `null` or an error keeps the fallback (`fallbackSky`: cover 0.6, high 0.5, the pre-weather look); the Worker never answers 5xx, so live there is no console output. `?weather=clear|partly|overcast|rain|snow|fog|storm` forces a preset (`weatherPresets`) and skips the fetch, for tests. Crawlers (owner call 2026-10-13, "for search engines always a bit cloudy night, stars"): without `?weather=`, a user agent that `isCrawler` (`src/lib/crawler.ts`, tested: search, AI and link preview bots, Lighthouse, headless browsers; `CUBOT` phones excluded) matches gets `crawlerSky` (the `partly` preset) at `nightPhase` (day, twilight and dusk 0), with no weather fetch and no clock refresh; text and markup stay the same for everyone, only the sky changes. An explicit `?weather=` wins, so the headless test scripts keep their presets and times. The image generator draws the same crawler sky (`crawlerSky` and `nightPhase`) with no puffs. Wind: `drift` is the real east-west wind (`-sign(east) x clamp(|east| / 15 km/h, 0.5, 2)`, 0.5 under 1 km/h), so the clouds move with the wind, east to the right; the fallback drifts left at 1x. Cloud motion uses two clocks: `u_cloudTime` only morphs the clouds slowly; `u_cloudShift` moves them. Every cloud feature, the cell warp and the bank height are read at `x + shift`, so the clouds move as rigid layers and never warp, shear or mix. Swipe gust: mouse, pen and touch movement in the lowest 0.8 of the screen adds gust energy (1.8 per screen height of travel, at most 1; on touch the up-down part counts a quarter, so a scroll gives a small push and a sideways drag the full one; touch is read from `touchmove`, because the browser cancels the pointer once it starts a scroll; a lifted finger starts fresh); the energy eases back over about 2.3 s, and the shift clock runs `drift - 2.5 x smoothstep(energy) x dir` times normal, so a swipe adds up to 2.5 times the base speed in the swipe's horizontal direction (3.5x at the fallback drift, up to 4.5x with a strong wind); a new gust starts in the wind's direction, so a straight up or down swipe speeds the clouds the way they already move. Swipe puffs (owner pick B1 "Swell", `puffMode` 0): the same movement in the lowest 0.55 of the screen drops a puff every 0.11 screen heights along the path (10 slots in `u_puffs`, a new one replaces the weakest; at most 0.3 above the bottom). A puff rises over 0.7 s to 0.9 and settles over about 4 s; it rides with its cloud layer and swells the bank around it (more cells, a taller top). It only adds cloud: a proof render showed 0 pixels losing cloud and at most 3/255 change outside the puff. `puffMode` 1 ("Condense", fresh cloud appears in clear sky) is the other pick. `u_puffOn` skips the puff loop when no puff is visible. The pointer never moves cloud shapes, and the far layer takes no pointer parallax. A stopped sky ignores pointer and touch movement (no gust, no puffs), and a hold, a pause or a hidden tab clears the gust and the puffs; the sky keeps full frame rate while the energy is above 0.01 or a puff is visible. Daylight: the sky follows the Vienna clock (owner choice 2026-10-12), not the visitor's (`u_daylight` = day, twilight, dusk from `skyPhase` in `src/lib/sky-clock.ts`, tested: the Europe/Vienna wall time from `Intl.DateTimeFormat`, sunrise and sunset at latitude 48.21 with the summer-time shift read from that time, refreshed every 30 s). By day the sky lifts toward azure, stars and the corona fade, and the clouds get lighter. Lower sky: owner pick C1 on 2026-10-12 in the claude.ai artifact "Lower Sky Lab" (<https://claude.ai/artifact/T7G7Zg3HXdSho7dWWPgH76>, version 3, pick `{"base":"B1","haze":0.21,"kelvin":2350,"city":0.65,"clouds":0.7,"precip":0.5,"light":0.87,"blue":1.6,"shade":1.18}`; change the look there first, then copy the values). By day the clear sky is Rayleigh plus aerosol single scattering with the Kasten-Young air mass (`airmass`, `clearSky`, `tauRay`, `aerosol`, `haze` 0.21 = aerosol optical depth at 550 nm, `skyFov` 0.873 rad), so the horizon is paler than the zenith; it is scaled to the gradient at mid-screen and fades in over the lowest 0.75 of the screen. Dawn and dusk draw a band of sunlight through a long air path (`sunTint`), only where the horizon is open (`openSky` = (1 - overcast) x (1 - 0.85 fog), squared). At night the city glows with a 2350 K lamp color (`lampColor`, `cityGlow` 0.65), amplified by clouds (x10.1, Kyba 2011), snow on the ground (x19, `1 + 18 x snow`) and fog, then a 0.33 power for perceived brightness, 55% desaturated (mesopic vision). There is no fixed pink horizon glow and no separate twilight band any more. Day light: `dayLift` 0.87 and `dayTint` 1.6 (lighter and bluer than the 0.52 and 0.8 of the "Sky Clock Lab" pick), `dayGain` 1.6731 (= 0.87 / 0.52) lifts the clouds by day and the overcast gray and the fog by its square root, the stars fade fully at noon, and the center shade by day is `dayShade` 1.18 (0.74 before). A shooting star every 2 to 14 s (1.45 s flight) starts above the screen center, hidden under overcast (the post header has its own fixed path, see Sky modes); Pixel-sized details (star cores, rim, flash, snowflakes) are scaled by `u_labPx` to match the lab at 1.2 lab pixels per CSS pixel. It renders at 0.75x (then 0.5x, then 0.35x without clouds on slow frames), at most 1.4 M pixels, caps at about 90 fps, drops to 30 fps after 800 ms without input (a gust or a puff keeps it at full rate until it eases back), pauses when hidden or blurred, freezes after eight frames in a row over 100 ms, and draws one still frame under reduced motion and on a software renderer (`src/lib/webgl.ts`: SwiftShader, llvmpipe, Microsoft Basic Render). Its `u_time` starts at 0 and wraps after a day of running time (1024 s without `highp`); it runs on an accumulated clock (`createFrameClock` in `src/lib/frame-clock.ts`, tested: the sum of frame steps, each capped at 100 ms and never negative; `pause()` on every stop and right before every draw made while stopped, such as resize and scroll frames), so after a pause, a hold or a hidden tab it resumes where it stopped. The shooting star uses its own progress value. There is no separate cloud canvas and no `sky:pulse` event since 2026-10-12.
- Corona layer (owner call 2026-10-13, "the rays are glitchy on the scrolling"): the sky canvas is fixed and redrawn on the main thread, so a corona drawn in it lags the portrait during a compositor scroll. When the page can scroll (`scrollHeight > clientHeight`, so phones, tablets and short desktop windows; desktop no-scroll sizes keep the single canvas), `SpaceBackground` also draws the corona into `[data-corona] > canvas`: an `aria-hidden` div in `body` (`position: absolute; inset: 0; z-index: -2; overflow: clip; mix-blend-mode: screen`, after the sky canvas and under `body::before`, `visibility: hidden` until `data-live` is set), so it scrolls with the portrait on the compositor and never widens the page. It is a second WebGL context running `coronaFragmentShader` (the same `skyCommon` GLSL with its own `main`) over a box of 7.6 portrait radii around the portrait, clipped to the body, at the sky canvas pixel pitch (`placeLayer` in `src/lib/corona-layer.ts`, tested): at rest the box snaps to the sky pixel grid, while the page scrolls it keeps its page position and only `u_origin` (the sky pixel of its bottom-left) moves, so the rays keep their sample phase. Both programs get the same uniforms each frame through `fanOut`. The split is exact by math: the tone map `1 - exp(-1.15 x)` of a sum is the `screen` blend of the two tone-mapped parts, so the layer writes the corona alone through the same darkening, fog, vignette, brightness, hue turn and tone map, and the sky canvas (`u_split = 1`) leaves it out. `layerKeep` hands the corona back to the sky canvas where `screen` cannot be exact: beyond 6 radii (fades out by 7.5, only the smooth low-octave glow is left there), under the cloud tops (`cloudTop` x `1.3 + 0.35 bump`, plus a 0.05 band) and under rain shafts (`yb < 0.42`); both shaders compute the same mask, so the seams only show as a soft cross-fade while a scroll is in flight. With the layer live the sky canvas also skips the dark disc it draws under the photo, and `[data-portrait]` carries `--background` instead (it scrolls with the page, so the spot stays dark before the image decodes and while the portrait dialog hides the card photo); nothing in the sky canvas then follows the page, so a scroll needs no sky frame. Without the layer the disc stays and a scroll redraws as before. The placement key is rounded to 1/64 px, so the float noise of a fractional phone scroll never re-snaps the layer mid-scroll. By day the corona in the layer is not rolled off (the day roll-off below runs on the sky canvas only); near the portrait the day sky sits under the knee, so the difference stays small. At rest the composite matches the single canvas within the screenshot noise (mean under 0.15/255) except a 1 to 2 px ring at the portrait edge (up to about 35/255 with the card hidden), where separate bilinear upscaling of the two canvases differs from upscaling their blend; the portrait and its ring cover most of it. The software renderer gets no layer, and a lost layer context hides it, so the sky canvas draws the corona as before. `scripts/generate-images.ts` leaves `u_split` at 0 and draws the corona in one pass.
- No renderer blocks on shader compiles: the planets link their programs with `KHR_parallel_shader_compile` and poll before the first draw (`whenLinked` in `planet-gl.ts`; the context options, the software-renderer check, the clear state, the canvas size, the frame loop and the noise GLSL are shared by the repo planets and the blog moons through `planet-stage.ts` and `planet-gl.ts`, link errors checked only outside production); the globe, the only three.js user (a lazy chunk loaded on its click), compiles with `renderer.compileAsync` and sets `renderer.debug.checkShaderErrors` only outside production. The planet port (2026-10-12) kept every GLSL body, the geometry, the sRGB-to-linear uniform colors, the draw states and order, and took about 104 KB brotli off the first visit (0 pixel diff on the still frame). Measured with `~/powertools/scripts/site-probe.mjs`: no blocking GL calls, startup long tasks fell from 955 ms to 67 ms.
- Sky modes (`SpaceBackground` `mode`, `src/lib/sky-preset.ts`): `home` is the card sky and is unchanged. `index` (`/blog`) is full screen with live weather, the corona layer and `blogIndexInkTargets`, and it is the only mode that listens for the `sky:preset` event. `post` mounts as the first child of `header[data-sky-header]` (no portal, so the canvas is in the static HTML), covers the header, fades out over its lowest 30%, pauses off screen, has no weather fetch, no clock refresh and no corona layer, and uses `postInkTargets`. Post mode draws the LinkedIn cover rays (owner pick A3, 2026-10-13): `longRaysShader` from `src/lib/sky-rays.ts` (the shader with `coronaSize` 1.6 and `coronaBright` 1.3, made by `patchShader`, the same copy `scripts/generate-images.ts` draws the cover with; the home shader is untouched) with the corona source off canvas at the top-left (`cornerCorona(canvas.height)`: center (-r, -r), r = 0.43 x the header height), and a shooting star on `cornerMeteor` (x 0.3, y 0.38, angle -0.4, x pulled left on narrow headers to `halfWidth - 0.55`), 1.6 s every 6 s, only while the sky runs and never for crawlers or under full overcast. A preset is `{ phase, weather }`; `setSkyPreset(preset | null)` stores it in `latestSkyPreset()` and dispatches `sky:preset` (`detail` is the preset or `null`), and the index sky eases weather and hour over 3 s, `null` easing back to live. `startingSkyPreset(mode, crawler)` returns the remembered preset only for the index and never for a crawler. Whoever calls `setSkyPreset(post.sky)` must call `setSkyPreset(null)` on leave and unmount (see `blog.md`). The full description is in the **Blog** bullet "Sky modes" of `docs/architecture.md`.
