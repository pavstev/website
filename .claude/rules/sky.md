# Sky, planets and shaders

Loads when you touch the sky, the planets, the logo planets or any WebGL helper. Sibling rules: `card.md`, `motion.md`, `globe.md`.

- Logo planets: `product-mark.tsx` renders `span.logo-planet[data-logo]` with an `aria-hidden` inline `svg` (a disc `circle`, then a `g[transform]` of `path[data-ink="mark"|"accent"]`) filled from the inline `--logo-disc`, `--logo-mark` and `--logo-accent`. That flat mark is the fallback (no JS, no WebGL, software renderer, Save-Data, context loss, forced colors, print) and the source the canvas reads, so the path data ships once. `RepoPlanetsCanvas` sits in `.card-strips` and uses its parent as the stage; `repo-planets.ts` finds `.repo-planet` and `.logo-planet` nodes and sets `data-planets="gl"` on the stage itself, and `@media screen and (forced-colors: none)` hides `[data-planets="gl"] .logo-planet svg` with a 300 ms fade once the first frame (the sunrise start pose) is drawn. `createLogoPlanet` (`src/lib/logo-planet.ts`) draws the paths with `Path2D` into a 256x256 mask (mark ink red, accent ink green, `lighter`; the `<g>` transform applied by hand, as `Path2D` ignores ancestor transforms), uploads it as a texture (no color conversion, anisotropy `min(4, max)`, mipmaps, the settings three.js used before the port) and draws it with one shared logo program: the planet lighting with the mask printed on the front hemisphere only, the logo faintly visible on the night side. A missing color or mask throws, and the whole strip falls back to CSS. Repo and logo planets use the fixed view vector `vec3(0, 0, 1)` (orthographic camera), so a rim does not depend on the tile position. Motion (`src/lib/logo-motion.ts`, pure, tested): a sunrise once per page view from -0.9 rad (turned away, at the terminator, so the mark is in view almost at once) to 0, ease-out cubic, 1.6 s, starting the first time the logo is 60% visible while the canvas runs (one `IntersectionObserver` per logo), and never before the first frame is drawn, so the first frame shows the start pose even while the shaders still compile; then a plus or minus 0.18 rad wobble (period about 9 s, never a full turn), small enough that the whole mark stays inside the disc. Hover or `:focus-visible` in the tile spins it up like the repo planets (speed x3.4, at most 0.28 rad, the `uHot` glow). A held or paused scene, a freeze or reduced motion snaps an unfinished sunrise to the rest pose with one draw; a hidden tab or an off-screen stage resumes and finishes it. Reduced motion draws one still frame at the rest pose.
- Sky (`space-background.tsx`, `data-space-state`: `running`, `paused`, `static`, `fallback`): one raw WebGL fragment shader (the GLSL lives in `src/lib/sky-shader.ts`, shared with the image generator) with the "Eclipse" look the owner tuned on 2026-10-12 in the claude.ai artifact "Eclipse Lab" (knob values baked in as shader constants; change the look there first, then copy the values). The canvas is `position: absolute; inset: 0` inside `body` (`position: relative`, `display: flow-root`, so the dock's bottom margin stays inside and the canvas covers the whole page). It scrolls with the page, so the corona never lags behind the portrait and a scroll needs no redraw. Shader units are the viewport height (`u_view`) and the page bottom is the horizon: on pages that scroll, the horizon glow and the low clouds sit at the end of the page. The corona, pink flares, a thin rim and a diamond-ring flash (about every 7.7 s) are centered on `[data-card] [data-portrait]`, read with `getBoundingClientRect` every frame (so intro transforms and layout changes follow) and never moved by the pointer; only the stars take a small pointer parallax on fine pointers. Low clouds (owner pick 2026-10-12 in the claude.ai artifact "Cloud and Star Lab", look A3 made realistic): a stratocumulus bank in two layers up to about a third of a viewport above the page bottom. Each layer is rows of irregular cells (a jittered cell grid, warped by fBm, rows flatter and denser toward the horizon, more gaps higher up, a solid stratus band at the bottom) with fBm-frayed edges. Light is a two-sample march: the density a step below a point (toward the horizon glow) sets the orange underlight, the density a step above sets the sky light on the tops, and thin parts let the glow through. Thin high wisps sit between 0.26 and 0.8 viewport. Stars grow denser toward the page top (`starTop` 1.7 at the top, today's density below 0.3 of the page; the big star layer takes the least of it). Stars behind the bio (`[data-card] .bio`, read every frame like the portrait, passed as `u_quiet`) shine at 35%, so the denser stars never break the 6.9:1 bio contrast; this was needed on phones, where the bio sits high on a tall page. Cloud speed-up: two clocks. `u_cloudTime` only morphs the clouds slowly; `u_cloudShift` moves them. Every cloud feature, the cell warp and the bank height are read at `x + shift`, so the clouds move as rigid layers and never warp, shear or mix. Mouse, pen and touch movement in the lowest 0.8 viewport of the page adds gust energy (1.8 per viewport of travel, at most 1; on touch the up-down part counts a quarter, so a scroll gives a small push and a sideways drag the full one; touch is read from `touchmove`, because the browser cancels the pointer once it starts a scroll; a lifted finger starts fresh); the energy eases back over about 2.3 s, and the shift clock runs `1 - 2.5 * energy * dir` times normal, so a swipe speeds the drift up to 3.5x in the swipe's horizontal direction (the base drift runs left). The pointer never moves cloud shapes, and the far layer takes no pointer parallax. A hold, a pause or a hidden tab clears the energy; the sky keeps full frame rate while the energy is above 0.01. The corona streamers turn around the portrait (about one turn per 38 s), the fine rays turn slowly the other way and the flares drift with them. Daylight: the sky follows the visitor's local clock (`u_daylight` = day, twilight, dusk from `skyPhase` in `src/lib/sky-clock.ts`, tested; sunrise and sunset from the date at latitude 47 with the summer-time shift, refreshed every 30 s). By day the sky lifts toward azure, stars and the corona fade, and the clouds get lighter; dawn glows rose and dusk amber. The owner tuned these values on 2026-10-12 in the claude.ai artifact "Sky Clock Lab"; change them there first. The bio text keeps at least 6.9:1 against the sky at every hour (measured at 1440x900). A shooting star every 2 to 14 s (1.45 s flight) starts above the current viewport center; any pointerdown sends a faint ripple. Pixel-sized details (star cores, rim, flash) are scaled by `u_labPx` to match the lab at 1.2 lab pixels per CSS pixel. It renders at 0.75x (then 0.5x, then 0.35x without clouds on slow frames), at most 1.4 M pixels, caps at about 90 fps, drops to 30 fps after 800 ms without input (a cloud gust keeps it at full rate until it eases back), pauses when hidden or blurred, freezes after eight frames in a row over 100 ms, and draws one still frame under reduced motion and on a software renderer (`src/lib/webgl.ts`: SwiftShader, llvmpipe, Microsoft Basic Render). Its `u_time` starts at 0 and wraps after a day of running time (1024 s without `highp`); it runs on an accumulated clock (`createFrameClock` in `src/lib/frame-clock.ts`, tested: the sum of frame steps, each capped at 100 ms and never negative; `pause()` on every stop and right before every draw made while stopped, such as resize frames), so after a pause, a hold or a hidden tab it resumes where it stopped. The shooting star uses its own progress value. There is no separate cloud canvas and no `sky:pulse` event since 2026-10-12.
- No renderer blocks on shader compiles: the planets link their programs with `KHR_parallel_shader_compile` and poll before the first draw (`whenLinked` in `planet-gl.ts`, link errors checked only outside production); the globe, the only three.js user (a lazy chunk loaded on its click), compiles with `renderer.compileAsync` and sets `renderer.debug.checkShaderErrors` only outside production. The planet port (2026-10-12) kept every GLSL body, the geometry, the sRGB-to-linear uniform colors, the draw states and order, and took about 104 KB brotli off the first visit (0 pixel diff on the still frame). Measured with `~/powertools/scripts/site-probe.mjs`: no blocking GL calls, startup long tasks fell from 955 ms to 67 ms.
