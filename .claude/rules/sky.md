# Sky, planets and shaders

Loads when you touch the sky, the planets, the logo planets or any WebGL helper. Sibling rules: `card.md`, `motion.md`, `globe.md`.

- Logo planets: `product-mark.tsx` renders `span.logo-planet[data-logo]` with an `aria-hidden` inline `svg` (a disc `circle`, then a `g[transform]` of `path[data-ink="mark"|"accent"]`) filled from the inline `--logo-disc`, `--logo-mark` and `--logo-accent`. That flat mark is the fallback (no JS, no WebGL, software renderer, Save-Data, context loss, forced colors, print) and the source the canvas reads, so the path data ships once. `RepoPlanetsCanvas` sits in each drawer's `.drawer-stage` (see Drawers in `card.md`), uses its parent as the stage, boots when its drawer opens and disposes when it closes; `repo-planets.ts` finds `.repo-planet` and `.logo-planet` nodes and sets `data-planets="gl"` on the stage itself, and `@media screen and (forced-colors: none)` hides `[data-planets="gl"] .logo-planet svg` with a 300 ms fade once the first frame (the sunrise start pose) is drawn. `createLogoPlanet` (`src/lib/logo-planet.ts`) draws the paths with `Path2D` into a 256x256 mask (mark ink red, accent ink green, `lighter`; the `<g>` transform applied by hand, as `Path2D` ignores ancestor transforms), uploads it as a texture (no color conversion, anisotropy `min(4, max)`, mipmaps, the settings three.js used before the port) and draws it with one shared logo program: the planet lighting with the mask printed on the front hemisphere only, the logo faintly visible on the night side. A missing color or mask throws, and the whole strip falls back to CSS. Repo and logo planets use the fixed view vector `vec3(0, 0, 1)` (orthographic camera), so a rim does not depend on the tile position. Motion (`src/lib/logo-motion.ts`, pure, tested): a sunrise once per page view from -0.9 rad (turned away, at the terminator, so the mark is in view almost at once) to 0, ease-out cubic, 1.6 s, starting the first time the logo is 60% visible while the canvas runs (one `IntersectionObserver` per logo), and never before the first frame is drawn, so the first frame shows the start pose even while the shaders still compile; then a plus or minus 0.18 rad wobble (period about 9 s, never a full turn), small enough that the whole mark stays inside the disc. Hover or `:focus-visible` in the tile spins it up like the repo planets (speed x3.4, at most 0.28 rad, the `uHot` glow). A held or paused scene, a freeze or reduced motion snaps an unfinished sunrise to the rest pose with one draw; a hidden tab or an off-screen stage resumes and finishes it. Reduced motion draws one still frame at the rest pose.
- Sky (`space-background.tsx`, `data-space-state`: `running`, `paused`, `static`, `fallback`), home mode: the ground-level Vienna sky from Sky Lab v3 (owner pick Q29 on 2026-10-13, claude.ai artifact <https://claude.ai/artifact/LR9SsBC56VjEJXUiKUZFgT>, frame "Sun path in frame" from the Upper Belvedere, heading 352). One WebGL context (`depth: true`, `antialias: true`) draws two programs per frame: the sky fragment shader `homeSkyFragmentShader` (`src/lib/sky-shader.ts`, the shared `skyCommon` GLSL plus the lab functions, knob values baked in as constants: cloud base 1500 m, tops 2500 m, reach 0.62, cell 1.8 km, silver lining 0.6, 7 ray steps, glow 1.6, park 1450 m, visibility 16 km, cap ceiling 0.17, no center dim, Rayleigh over the full height) and then the skyline (below). The canvas is `position: fixed; bottom: 0` with `height: 100lvh`. Camera: a pinhole at 40 m over the city floor, 50 degrees vertical (`cameraFor` in `src/lib/sky-scene.ts`), the horizon at 13% of the screen height from the bottom (9% when the screen is taller than wide, `horizonShare` in `src/lib/sky-light.ts`); `camRay` turns each pixel into a ray, so clouds, cirrus, rain shafts and swipe puffs live in world space and shrink toward the horizon. Below the horizon `ground` draws the city floor with fog by distance, a dark park in front (so the footer sits on dark ground), street lamp lines, car lights on an avenue and the Ring, scattered lamps, wet and snowy ground (`u_ground`, from the eased rain and snow). Clouds: perspective cumulus (`cumulus`, a short march through a slab, height field from fBm plus up to 6 world puffs `u_wpuff`, Henyey-Greenstein silver lining toward the shown sun, moonlight on the edges) and cirrus at 8.5 km; they ride the wind in metres (`u_wind` = 25 m per shift second x (1, 0.3) for the cumulus and (1.8, 0.4) for the cirrus, the shift clock is the old `drift` and swipe gust clock, wrapped at 7200 s on `highp`). The sky rolls off toward 0.17 linear luminance by day, and by night above the horizon where there are clouds or overcast (weighted by `max(ov, cloudA)`, so the stars and the shooting star keep their sparkle and the city lamps below the horizon stay bright), never on the sun and moon disks (`bodyMask`). The old stratocumulus bank, the center dim, the portrait corona (Q23) and the corona layer are gone from home; the blog keeps them through `skyFragmentShader` (see Sky modes). Stars sit above the horizon only and take the camera look times 0.6 as parallax. Sun and moon (Q24, Q29): `src/lib/sky-ephemeris.ts` (Meeus, tested) gives the true positions, the moon phase, the bright limb and the parallactic angle at Vienna (48.2082, 16.3738); `src/lib/sky-path.ts` (tested) maps them to a path in the frame, so the sun shows from its rise to its set and the moon whenever it is up, the day moon too: rise at the right edge, set at the left, the peak over the card when there is room above it (`above`), else in the band under it (`below`), else behind it; the card box is the card height and the bio's text width. `skyLight` (`src/lib/sky-light.ts`, pure, tested) turns the instant, the weather and that layout into every light uniform: the day phase from the true sun altitude (`phaseFromSun`, replaces the old Vienna-clock `skyPhase` on home), the sun color through the air mass, sky and ground ambient, city under-light, the sun disk, glare and air-mass flattening, the moon brightness, halo, earthshine and the tilt mirrored like the lab's squeeze frame. The sky glow, the cloud silver lining and the glare follow the shown disk (`u_sun.xyz`); the band tint and the ground light follow the true altitude (`u_sun.w`); the buildings take the true sun direction plus a rim light from the shown one. The glare and moon halo under the name, bio and footer are capped at 0.3 (`textCap`, `u_txt` from the probe's text boxes). `skyLight` reruns every 30 s, on resize, 1.5 s after start (the intro has settled) and when a weather answer arrives. `?at=<ISO instant>` pins the time (`instantOverride`), like `?weather=`. Crawlers get a fixed moonless night (`crawlerInstant`, 2026-01-20 23:00 CET) with `crawlerSky`. Pointer: on fine pointers the camera turns up to 1.5 degrees of yaw and 0.5 of pitch toward the pointer (eased at rate 4); reduced motion, pause and a stopped sky keep it still. Swipe gust and puffs work as before, but a puff is placed on the cloud base where the pointer ray meets it (`worldPuff`, at most 6, rise 0.7 s to 0.9, settle over 4 s). Text over the sky (owner calls 2026-10-13: no boxes, no shadows): six `text-sky` texts with `inkTargets` in `src/lib/sky-ink.ts` (name 3, bio 4.5 since the sky port, copyright 4.5), `text-sky` is `color-mix(in srgb, var(--ink), white calc(var(--lift) * 100%))`, and `createSkyProbe` (`src/lib/sky-probe.ts`) draws the sky shader into a 24x6 sample grid per text box through `u_probe`, reads it back one frame later, and `skyLevel`, `liftFor` and `nextLift` pick the lift (98th percentile plus 0.02, steps of 0.05, target x 1.35, raise at once, lower past 0.12). The probe sees the sky and the ground, not the buildings. Weather mapping (`src/lib/weather.ts`, `fallbackSky`, `weatherPresets`, `?weather=`, the 15 minute `/weather` fetch in production only, the 3 s ease, the crawler rule) is unchanged; `u_weather` and `u_fall` feed the cover, overcast deck, fog, rain streaks and shafts, snow and lightning as before. Frame pacing is unchanged: 0.75x (then 0.5x, then 0.35x without clouds), at most 1.4 M pixels, about 90 fps, 30 fps after 800 ms idle, pause when hidden or blurred, freeze after eight frames over 100 ms, one still frame under reduced motion and on a software renderer (`src/lib/webgl.ts`), `createFrameClock` for `u_time`.
- Text limit: behind the same three text boxes (64 px feather at 900 px tall) `capText` in the shared `textShade` GLSL rolls the sky luminance off above 0.10 toward 0.16 (linear) in every phase, just under the day roll-off (0.17), so no box shows on a bright day sky; with the text lifted to white that still gives 4.5:1. The skyline is held to 0.11 there (`textUnder(fog)` in `skyline-gl.ts` keeps the blended building plus fogged sky under it, since the probe does not see the buildings; city light points fade out there). It only darkens bright spots such as the lit cloud band at night or the windows behind the footer on phones; a dark sky is untouched. The contrast audit found 70 readings under target without it (2026-10-13).
- Skyline (Q2, Q20, Q30): `src/lib/skyline.ts` (pure, tested) builds the city once, on idle after the first sky frame (`requestIdleCallback`, 2 s timeout; about 45 ms, 136 k vertices of 12 floats: position, normal, landmark id, material, wheel flag, random, edge code, floodlight): 16 landmarks at their true bearing and distance from the Upper Belvedere (Stephansdom, Riesenrad, Donauturm, DC Tower 1, Karlskirche, Rathaus, Millennium Tower, Hofburg, Votivkirche, Parlament, Uniqa Tower, Ares Tower, Spittelau, Ringturm, Lower Belvedere, Kunsthistorisches Museum, all at scale 1.1), the Kahlenberg ridge with its mast, and three rings of city blocks, sorted far to near. `src/lib/skyline-gl.ts` draws it in the sky's context with one `TRIANGLES` draw and one `POINTS` draw (red aviation lights, blinking, at night), depth test, back-face culling and the blend `ONE, SRC_ALPHA` (the fragment writes its color times 1 - fog and the fog as alpha). Shading lives in one GLSL function, `shadeBuilding`: materials, sun, sky and city under-light, specular, sky reflections, lit windows from dusk (34%, 30% cool), tower floor bands, floodlit landmarks, snow and wet facades, then the drawn "Ink line" look (Q30, lead pick at strength 0.6, `inkStyle`): tones banded in steps of 2^(1/1.5), a slight paper wash, a light diagonal hatch on the shadow side, and ink edges drawn from per-vertex edge codes (only real box and roof edges, never a quad diagonal; smooth domes and cylinders get a silhouette line from n·V instead), about 1.5 px wide through `OES_standard_derivatives` and faded out on faces smaller than about 4 px; without the extension there are no lines. The Riesenrad turns 4 times faster than real (one turn in about 64 s). On fine pointers a landmark under the pointer leans 1.5 degrees away and lifts 10 m (`pickLandmark` on the projected boxes, `easeLean` at rate 6), only when the topmost element there is `main`, `[data-card]` or the canvas, never under a control or card text. No skyline on a software renderer or with Save-Data. The skyline never draws in the blog mode.
- No renderer blocks on shader compiles: the planets link their programs with `KHR_parallel_shader_compile` and poll before the first draw (`whenLinked` in `planet-gl.ts`; the context options, the software-renderer check, the clear state, the canvas size, the frame loop and the noise GLSL are shared by the repo planets through `planet-stage.ts` and `planet-gl.ts`, link errors checked only outside production); the globe, the only three.js user (a lazy chunk loaded on its click), compiles with `renderer.compileAsync` and sets `renderer.debug.checkShaderErrors` only outside production. The planet port (2026-10-12) kept every GLSL body, the geometry, the sRGB-to-linear uniform colors, the draw states and order, and took about 104 KB brotli off the first visit (0 pixel diff on the still frame). Measured with `~/powertools/scripts/site-probe.mjs`: no blocking GL calls, startup long tasks fell from 955 ms to 67 ms.
- Sky modes (`SpaceBackground` `mode`, `src/lib/sky-preset.ts`): `home` is the ground-level sky above. `blog` is one sky for every blog page (owner calls 2026-10-13, Q7 and Q17): mounted once by `src/app/blog/layout.tsx` inside `[data-blog]`, a fixed `div[data-blog-sky]` (one screen plus `--sky-travel` 40vh) with the canvas inside, the clear night (`blogLook()`) with the LinkedIn cover rays (owner pick A3, 2026-10-13: `longRaysShader` from `src/lib/sky-rays.ts`, `coronaSize` 1.6 and `coronaBright` 1.3, made by `patchShader`, the same copy `scripts/generate-images.ts` draws the cover with; the home shader is untouched), the corona source off canvas at the top-left (`cornerCorona(view)`, r = 0.43 x the view, where `u_view` is 72% of the screen height, at most 960 CSS px, so the look matches the old 72svh post header), and a shooting star on `cornerMeteor` (y measured from the top of the view, x pulled left on narrow screens), 1.6 s every 6 s, only while the sky runs and never for crawlers. No weather fetch, no clock refresh, no corona layer, no `sky:preset` event, no per-post sky. Parallax runs on the compositor (`sky-drift`, a scroll-driven `translate` of the wrapper to -40vh over the first 100vh; one `requestAnimationFrame` per scroll where `animation-timeline` is missing), never as a shader redraw per scroll frame. A scroll-driven `[data-sky-fade]` layer fades the whole sky to 80% `--background` over the header height and `::after` darkens its lower part, so body text keeps at least 9.9:1. It runs while `[data-sky-sentinel]` (the first 50vh of the page) is in view and holds a still frame below; reduced motion, the pause button, a software renderer, Save-Data and crawlers get a still sky and no parallax (`data-sky-still` for crawlers and Save-Data). Ink targets: `postInkTargets` (equal to `blogIndexInkTargets`), the title and meta line in `[data-sky-header]`. The full description is in the **Blog** bullets "Sky modes" and "Blog chrome" of `docs/architecture.md`.
