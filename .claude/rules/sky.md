# Sky, planets and shaders

Loads when you touch the sky, the planets, the logo planets or any WebGL helper. Sibling rules: `card.md`, `motion.md`, `globe.md`.

- Logo planets: `product-mark.tsx` renders `span.logo-planet[data-logo]` with an `aria-hidden` inline `svg` (a disc `circle`, then a `g[transform]` of `path[data-ink="mark"|"accent"]`) filled from the inline `--logo-disc`, `--logo-mark` and `--logo-accent`. That flat mark is the fallback (no JS, no WebGL, software renderer, Save-Data, context loss, forced colors, print) and the source the canvas reads, so the path data ships once. `RepoPlanetsCanvas` sits in each drawer's `.drawer-stage` (see Drawers in `card.md`), uses its parent as the stage, boots when its drawer opens and disposes when it closes; `repo-planets.ts` finds `.repo-planet` and `.logo-planet` nodes and sets `data-planets="gl"` on the stage itself, and `@media screen and (forced-colors: none)` hides `[data-planets="gl"] .logo-planet svg` with a 300 ms fade once the first frame (the sunrise start pose) is drawn. `createLogoPlanet` (`src/lib/logo-planet.ts`) draws the paths with `Path2D` into a 256x256 mask (mark ink red, accent ink green, `lighter`; the `<g>` transform applied by hand, as `Path2D` ignores ancestor transforms), uploads it as a texture (no color conversion, anisotropy `min(4, max)`, mipmaps, the settings three.js used before the port) and draws it with one shared logo program: the planet lighting with the mask printed on the front hemisphere only, the logo faintly visible on the night side. A missing color or mask throws, and the whole strip falls back to CSS. Repo and logo planets use the fixed view vector `vec3(0, 0, 1)` (orthographic camera), so a rim does not depend on the tile position. Motion (`src/lib/logo-motion.ts`, pure, tested): a sunrise once per page view from -0.9 rad (turned away, at the terminator, so the mark is in view almost at once) to 0, ease-out cubic, 1.6 s, starting the first time the logo is 60% visible while the canvas runs (one `IntersectionObserver` per logo), and never before the first frame is drawn, so the first frame shows the start pose even while the shaders still compile; then a plus or minus 0.18 rad wobble (period about 9 s, never a full turn), small enough that the whole mark stays inside the disc. Hover or `:focus-visible` in the tile spins it up like the repo planets (speed x3.4, at most 0.28 rad, the `uHot` glow). A held or paused scene, a freeze or reduced motion snaps an unfinished sunrise to the rest pose with one draw; a hidden tab or an off-screen stage resumes and finishes it. Reduced motion draws one still frame at the rest pose.
- Sky (`space-background.tsx`, `data-space-state`: `running`, `paused`, `static`, `fallback`), home mode: the sky as you see it from the ground in Vienna, with no city (owner calls 2026-10-14: the skyline, the Ferris wheel, the aviation, street and car lights, the park and the city glow are gone; the Vienna globe on the card stays). One WebGL context (`contextOptions`, the same for both modes) draws the sky fragment shader `homeSkyFragmentShader` (`src/lib/sky-shader.ts`), then the star points (below). The canvas is `position: fixed; bottom: 0` with `height: 100lvh`. Below the horizon `ground` draws a plain dark field with distance fog, wet and snowy ground (`u_ground`), so the footer sits on dark ground.
- View (`src/lib/sky-view.ts`, pure, tested): the eye at 2 m, a straight horizon at 4% of the screen height from the bottom (`groundShare`), and a stereographic view centred on the horizon (pitch 0) that reaches the zenith at `top` of the sky part (`skyCamera`: 1 on a phone; on wide screens `1.8 x 0.96 x height / width`, so the side edges stay inside 0.9 of the stereographic unit). `projectSky` and `skyRay` are the forward and inverse maps; the GLSL `camRay` is the same inverse map. The world frame is east, up, north. `frameAim` picks what to face: the sun when it is up (the moon as a second body when it is up too), else the moon, else the twilight glow (the sun's azimuth while the sun is above -18 degrees), else where the next body rises in the next 36 h (10 min steps). Owner call: no fake body when both are down. `aimYaw` turns the view so the target sits in the middle of the free side next to the card (right first, left when only that side also shows the second body), or at 0.78 of the width when there is no room; a high body cannot move far from the middle, so the reach is capped at 0.9 cot(altitude). `aimHolds` keeps the yaw while the target stays on screen and off the card; a new kind, a target that leaves the frame, a resize and the settle at 1.5 s re-aim, and the view pans there at rate 1.6.
- Sun and moon (owner pick "Bigger disk", 2026-10-14): `src/lib/sky-ephemeris.ts` (Meeus, tested, within about 10 arcseconds of JPL Horizons above the horizon) gives the true positions, the phase, the bright limb and the parallactic angle at Vienna (48.2082, 16.3738). The disks are 3x their true size at their true centres; `diskGain` shrinks them to true size as the sun and moon close in (an eclipse), and the moon covers the sun. `skyLight(instant, weather)` (`src/lib/sky-light.ts`, pure, tested) turns the instant and the weather into every light uniform: the day phase from the true sun altitude (`phaseFromSun`), the sun color through the air mass, the ambient light, the sun disk, glare and air-mass flattening, the moon brightness, halo, earthshine and the crescent tilt. A body shows from -3 degrees up. Overcast never hides them: the sun disk keeps 25% (`veilFloor`), the moon 30% (`moonVeilFloor`). It reruns every 1 s, on resize, at the settle and when a weather answer arrives. `?at=<ISO instant>` pins the time, like `?weather=`. Crawlers get a fixed moonless night (`crawlerInstant`, 2026-01-20 23:00 CET) with `crawlerSky`.
- Stars (owner pick "Real stars", 2026-10-14): the Yale Bright Star Catalogue (BSC5, 9,096 stars, CDS VizieR V/50), packed by `pnpm stars:build <catalog.gz>` (`scripts/generate-star-data.ts`, proper motion applied to 2026.0) into `src/lib/star-data.ts` (base64; never edit it by hand). `src/lib/sky-frame.ts` builds the J2000 equatorial to local matrix (`equatorialToWorld`: IAU 1976 precession, nutation, apparent sidereal time) and the galactic matrix (`worldToGalactic`); both rerun every second. `src/lib/sky-planets.ts` (tested) places Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn from JPL's approximate elements (Standish, Table 1) with Mallama and Hilton 2018 magnitudes. `src/lib/star-gl.ts` draws stars and planets as one `POINTS` draw with additive blend after the sky quad; it loads as a lazy chunk on idle (2 s timeout) after the first frame and never under Save-Data. The vertex shader gives each star refraction, extinction (0.2 per air mass), cloud dimming from the same `cloudDepth` the clouds use, a sky brightness model (twilight, moon, the space fade, sun glare), Schaefer's naked-eye limit, twinkle that grows toward the horizon, and hides stars behind the moon. The Milky Way is procedural at its true place (`u_gal`, gain 0.2) and shows only in the dark.
- Space at the top (owner pick "Always some", 2026-10-14, not true to life by choice): `spaceMag` (`spaceGlsl`) fades the day sky toward a dark navy `spaceColor` above about 40 degrees, up to 10 magnitudes at the zenith, so a few bright stars show at noon. Overcast and fog close it. Earth shadow and the Belt of Venus show on the side away from the sun while it is 1 to 9 degrees below the horizon.
- Clouds: thin and see-through. `cloudGlsl` holds one stratiform layer at 2,600 m (cells 2.4 km): optical depth from the live cover and the slant, alpha 1 - exp(-depth), a two-lobe Henyey-Greenstein phase toward the sun and moon, one self-shadow sample, up to 6 world puffs (`u_wpuff`). Cirrus at 8.5 km also draws the 22 degree halo around the sun and moon. They ride the wind in metres (`u_wind`, the shift clock wrapped at 7200 s on `highp`). Rain days draw a grey veil and rain shafts, but the sun or moon still shows. The sky rolls off toward 0.17 linear luminance by day, and by night where there are clouds or overcast, never on the sun and moon disks (`bodyMask`). Pointer: on fine pointers the camera turns up to 1.5 degrees of yaw and 0.5 of pitch toward the pointer (eased at rate 4); reduced motion, pause and a stopped sky keep it still. A swipe puff sits on the cloud layer where the pointer ray meets it (`worldPuff` with `skyRay`, at most 6, rise 0.7 s to 0.9, settle over 4 s). Text over the sky (owner calls 2026-10-13: no boxes, no shadows): six `text-sky` texts with `inkTargets` in `src/lib/sky-ink.ts` (name 3, bio 4.5, copyright 4.5), `text-sky` is `color-mix(in srgb, var(--ink), white calc(var(--lift) * 100%))`, and `createSkyProbe` (`src/lib/sky-probe.ts`) draws the sky shader into a 24x6 sample grid per text box through `u_probe`, reads it back one frame later, and `skyLevel`, `liftFor` and `nextLift` pick the lift (98th percentile plus 0.02, steps of 0.05, target x 1.35, raise at once, lower past 0.12). The probe does not see the star points. Glare and moon halo under the text are capped at 0.3 (`textCap`, `u_txt`). Weather mapping (`src/lib/weather.ts`, `fallbackSky`, `weatherPresets`, `?weather=`, the 15 minute `/weather` fetch in production only, the 3 s ease, the crawler rule) is unchanged; `u_weather` and `u_fall` feed the cover, overcast deck, fog, rain streaks and shafts, snow and lightning. Frame pacing is unchanged: 0.75x (then 0.5x, then 0.35x without clouds), at most 1.4 M pixels, about 90 fps, 30 fps after 800 ms idle, pause when hidden or blurred, freeze after eight frames over 100 ms, one still frame under reduced motion and on a software renderer (`src/lib/webgl.ts`), `createFrameClock` for `u_time`.
- Text limit: behind the same three text boxes (64 px feather at 900 px tall) `capText` in the shared `textShade` GLSL rolls the sky luminance off above 0.10 toward 0.16 (linear) in every phase, just under the day roll-off (0.17), so no box shows on a bright day sky; with the text lifted to white that still gives 4.5:1. It only darkens bright spots such as a lit cloud or the sun glare near the name; a dark sky is untouched. The contrast audit found 70 readings under target without it (2026-10-13).
- No renderer blocks on shader compiles: the star points (`star-gl.ts`) poll `COMPLETION_STATUS_KHR` on a 16 ms timer and look up their attributes only once linked (an early `getAttribLocation` blocked the page 80 to 250 ms on a cold compile, measured 2026-10-14); the planets link their programs with `KHR_parallel_shader_compile` and poll before the first draw (`whenLinked` in `planet-gl.ts`; the context options, the software-renderer check, the clear state, the canvas size, the frame loop and the noise GLSL are shared by the repo planets through `planet-stage.ts` and `planet-gl.ts`, link errors checked only outside production); the globe, the only three.js user (a lazy chunk loaded on its click), compiles with `renderer.compileAsync` and sets `renderer.debug.checkShaderErrors` only outside production. The planet port (2026-10-12) kept every GLSL body, the geometry, the sRGB-to-linear uniform colors, the draw states and order, and took about 104 KB brotli off the first visit (0 pixel diff on the still frame). Measured with `~/powertools/scripts/site-probe.mjs`: no blocking GL calls, startup long tasks fell from 955 ms to 67 ms.
- Sky modes (`SpaceBackground` `mode`, `src/lib/sky-preset.ts`): `home` is the ground-level sky above. `blog` is one sky for every blog page (owner calls 2026-10-13, Q7 and Q17): mounted once by `src/app/blog/layout.tsx` inside `[data-blog]`, a fixed `div[data-blog-sky]` (one screen plus `--sky-travel` 40vh) with the canvas inside, the clear night (`blogLook()`) with the LinkedIn cover rays (owner pick A3, 2026-10-13: `longRaysShader` from `src/lib/sky-rays.ts`, `coronaSize` 1.6 and `coronaBright` 1.3, made by `patchShader`, the same copy `scripts/generate-images.ts` draws the cover with; the home shader is untouched), the corona source off canvas at the top-left (`cornerCorona(view)`, r = 0.43 x the view, where `u_view` is 72% of the screen height, at most 960 CSS px, so the look matches the old 72svh post header), and a shooting star on `cornerMeteor` (y measured from the top of the view, x pulled left on narrow screens), 1.6 s every 6 s, only while the sky runs and never for crawlers. No weather fetch, no clock refresh, no corona layer, no `sky:preset` event, no per-post sky. Parallax runs on the compositor (`sky-drift`, a scroll-driven `translate` of the wrapper to -40vh over the first 100vh; one `requestAnimationFrame` per scroll where `animation-timeline` is missing), never as a shader redraw per scroll frame. A scroll-driven `[data-sky-fade]` layer fades the whole sky to 80% `--background` over the header height and `::after` darkens its lower part, so body text keeps at least 9.9:1. It runs while `[data-sky-sentinel]` (the first 50vh of the page) is in view and holds a still frame below; reduced motion, the pause button, a software renderer, Save-Data and crawlers get a still sky and no parallax (`data-sky-still` for crawlers and Save-Data). Ink targets: `postInkTargets` (equal to `blogIndexInkTargets`), the title and meta line in `[data-sky-header]`. The full description is in the **Blog** bullets "Sky modes" and "Blog chrome" of `docs/architecture.md`.
