# Sky, planets and shaders

Loads when you touch the sky, the planets, the logo planets or any WebGL helper. Sibling rules: `card.md`, `motion.md`, `globe.md`.

- Logo planets: `product-mark.tsx` renders `span.logo-planet[data-logo]` with an `aria-hidden` inline `svg` (a disc `circle`, then a `g[transform]` of `path[data-ink="mark"|"accent"]`) filled from the inline `--logo-disc`, `--logo-mark` and `--logo-accent`. That flat mark is the fallback (no JS, no WebGL, software renderer, Save-Data, context loss, forced colors, print) and the source the canvas reads, so the path data ships once. `RepoPlanetsCanvas` sits in each drawer's `.drawer-stage` (see Drawers in `card.md`), uses its parent as the stage, boots when its drawer opens and disposes when it closes; `repo-planets.ts` finds `.repo-planet` and `.logo-planet` nodes and sets `data-planets="gl"` on the stage itself, and `@media screen and (forced-colors: none)` hides `[data-planets="gl"] .logo-planet svg` with a 300 ms fade once the first frame (the sunrise start pose) is drawn. `createLogoPlanet` (`src/lib/logo-planet.ts`) draws the paths with `Path2D` into a 256x256 mask (mark ink red, accent ink green, `lighter`; the `<g>` transform applied by hand, as `Path2D` ignores ancestor transforms), uploads it as a texture (no color conversion, anisotropy `min(4, max)`, mipmaps, the settings three.js used before the port) and draws it with one shared logo program: the planet lighting with the mask printed on the front hemisphere only, the logo faintly visible on the night side. A missing color or mask throws, and the whole strip falls back to CSS. Repo and logo planets use the fixed view vector `vec3(0, 0, 1)` (orthographic camera), so a rim does not depend on the tile position. Motion (`src/lib/logo-motion.ts`, pure, tested): a sunrise once per page view from -0.9 rad (turned away, at the terminator, so the mark is in view almost at once) to 0, ease-out cubic, 1.6 s, starting the first time the logo is 60% visible while the canvas runs (one `IntersectionObserver` per logo), and never before the first frame is drawn, so the first frame shows the start pose even while the shaders still compile; then a plus or minus 0.18 rad wobble (period about 9 s, never a full turn), small enough that the whole mark stays inside the disc. Hover or `:focus-visible` in the tile spins it up like the repo planets (speed x3.4, at most 0.28 rad, the `uHot` glow). A held or paused scene, a freeze or reduced motion snaps an unfinished sunrise to the rest pose with one draw; a hidden tab or an off-screen stage resumes and finishes it. Reduced motion draws one still frame at the rest pose.
- Sky (`space-background.tsx`, `data-space-state`: `running`, `paused`, `static`, `fallback`), home mode: the Vienna sky seen from 100 km up, the edge of space (owner calls 2026-10-14: the eye is high, there is no ground strip, the city and its lights stay gone; the Vienna globe on the card stays). One WebGL context (`contextOptions`, the same for both modes) draws the sky fragment shader `homeSkyFragmentShader` (`src/lib/sky-shader.ts`), then the star points (below). The canvas is `position: fixed; bottom: 0` with `height: 100lvh`. There is no `ground()` and no dark field: the bottom of the screen shows the curved Earth (`earthColor`: land, the cloud deck, haze and a thin lit atmosphere ring along the limb), the rest is the air above it and black space. The limb is antialiased with `cover`, so it is a clean curve, not a strip. Stars, the Milky Way, the sun and the moon sit behind it and are hidden where the Earth covers them.
- View (`src/lib/sky-view.ts`, pure, tested): the eye at `eyeAltitude` 100,000 m over a sphere of `earthRadius` 6,371,000 m, so the limb dips `limbDip` = acos(R / (R + h)), about 10.09 degrees, below the horizontal plane. `skyCamera(width, height)` is a stereographic camera that puts the limb at 16% of the screen height from the bottom (`limbShare`) as a straight line at pitch 0 (the curve comes from the maths, not a drawn ground) and reaches the zenith at `top` (1 on a phone; on wide screens `2 x 0.9 x 0.96 x height / width`, so the side edges stay inside 0.9 of the stereographic unit); the returned `horizon` is the screen share of the horizontal plane. `projectSky` and `skyRay` are the forward and inverse maps; the GLSL `camRay` is the same inverse map. The world frame is east, up, north. A body counts as up while its altitude plus its angular radius is above the limb (`-limbDip`), so the sun and moon stay visible a little past the ground sunset, as they do from orbit. `frameAim` picks what to face: the sun when it is up (the moon as a second body when it is up too), else the moon, else the twilight glow (the sun's azimuth while the sun is above -18 degrees), else where the next body rises in the next 36 h (10 min steps, `nextRise`). Owner call: no fake body when both are down. `aimYaw` turns the view so the target sits in the middle of the free side next to the card (right first, left when only that side also shows the second body), or at 0.78 of the width when there is no room; a high body cannot move far from the middle, so the reach is capped at 0.9 cot(altitude). `aimHolds` keeps the yaw while the target stays on screen (`onScreen`, with a `topMargin` of 24 px at the top) and off the card; a new kind, a target that leaves the frame, a resize and the settle at 1.5 s re-aim, and the view pans there at rate 1.6.
- Sun and moon (owner pick "Bigger disk", 2026-10-14): `src/lib/sky-ephemeris.ts` (Meeus, tested, within about 10 arcseconds of JPL Horizons above the horizon) gives the true positions, the phase, the bright limb and the parallactic angle at Vienna (48.2082, 16.3738). The disks are 7.5x their true size at their true centres (owner call 2026-10-14: bigger, `diskGainMax`); `diskGain` shrinks them to true size as the sun and moon close in (an eclipse), and the moon covers the sun. The sun has animated rays and a halo (owner calls 2026-10-14): in `sunAndMoon`, thin sparse rays whose angle is measured in screen space (`sunPx` is the sun projected like the star shader does, `dir = normalize(fc - sunPx)`; an angle taken on the sphere bends the rays into arcs in the wide stereographic view, which the owner called warped), each ray with its own reach (1 to 20 disk radii from a slow noise), noise over the unit circle (no seam) that drifts with `u_time`, a soft flicker, a warm to cool tint with distance, and a smooth three-lobe halo (0.45, 2 and 8 disk radii). They are not cut by `textCap`: the text limit (`capText`) and the dimmed stars keep the words readable. `skyLight(instant)` (`src/lib/sky-light.ts`, pure, tested) turns the instant into every light uniform; there is no weather input, and from up here the sun is never veiled. It gives the day phase from the true sun altitude (`phaseFromSun`), the sun color through the air mass along the path to the limb (`pathMass(elevation)`, 2 x `horizonMass` at and below the limb; `sunTint` stays white until the sun is near the limb), the sun disk, glare and flattening at the limb, the moon brightness, halo, earthshine and the crescent tilt. A body shows while it is above the limb (`sunShown`, `moonShown`). It reruns every 1 s, on resize and at the settle. `?at=<ISO instant>` pins the time, like `?weather=`. Crawlers get a fixed moonless night (`crawlerInstant`, 2026-01-20 23:00 CET) with `crawlerSky`.
- Stars (owner pick "Real stars", 2026-10-14): the Yale Bright Star Catalogue (BSC5, 9,096 stars, CDS VizieR V/50), packed by `pnpm stars:build <catalog.gz>` (`scripts/generate-star-data.ts`, proper motion applied to 2026.0) into `src/lib/star-data.ts` (base64; never edit it by hand). `src/lib/sky-frame.ts` builds the J2000 equatorial to local matrix (`equatorialToWorld`: IAU 1976 precession, nutation, apparent sidereal time) and the galactic matrix (`worldToGalactic`); both rerun every second. `src/lib/sky-planets.ts` (tested) places Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn from JPL's approximate elements (Standish, Table 1) with Mallama and Hilton 2018 magnitudes. `src/lib/star-gl.ts` draws stars and planets as one `POINTS` draw with additive blend after the sky quad; it loads as a lazy chunk on idle (2 s timeout) after the first frame and never under Save-Data. The vertex shader takes the eye altitude from `u_cam.z` and gives each star (owner call 2026-10-14: more, bigger, livelier: day sky penalty 3.2 magnitudes, points 4 to 10 px with a soft glow, a twinkle that never fully stops, a soft four-point glint on the brightest stars (`v_shine`, bigger sprite, off near text), and a dimming of up to 72% inside 56 px of the three text boxes through `u_txt`, as the probe does not see the star points; without it the night contrast audit failed at 3.46): Earth occlusion (a star below the deck limb is hidden), extinction along the real path (0.2 per air mass, the tangent path through the atmosphere near the limb, the column thinned by the 8 km scale height at the eye), a sky brightness model (twilight, moon, the day fade, sun glare), Schaefer's naked-eye limit, twinkle that grows with the air mass, and a mask behind the moon. The stars know no clouds any more: the deck is below the eye. The Milky Way is procedural at its true place (`u_gal`, gain 0.2) and shows in the dark.
- Space is real now (owner pick 100 km, 2026-10-14; the old "Always some" fade is gone): at 100 km the daytime sky above the limb is black with a thin blue atmosphere ring (`skyColor`, `airLight`, `scatter`: Rayleigh and Mie from an exponential column with a scale height of 8 km, tangent-point lighting, `skyMass`, `nearShare`), a faint green airglow band at 95 km (`smoothstep(0.02, -0.04, rd.y)`, intensity 0.22), the Earth shadow and the Belt of Venus on the side away from the sun at dusk, and the Milky Way. Stars show by day too, because the sky is dark. The whole screen rolls off toward 0.17 linear luminance (`homeCeiling`).
- Clouds from above (no live weather, owner call 2026-10-14): one stratiform deck at R + 2,600 m and cirrus at R + 8,500 m, both intersected as spheres (`shellHit`, `surfaceCoords`) and seen from above with `layerDensity` (a warped fbm; the cell size grows with the altitude, `1 + 7 x smoothstep(5000, 120000, alt)` in `sky-scene.ts` `deckScale`, `deckCell` 2.4 km) and `cirrusDensity`; self-shadow, a two-lobe Henyey-Greenstein phase toward the sun, a fixed look from `earthClouds` (`src/lib/sky-preset.ts`: cover 0.6, high cirrus 0.5, overcast 0, fog 0) fed to `u_weather` in home mode; the blog shader gets its own clear look from `blogLook`. They ride the wind in metres (`u_wind`, the shift clock wrapped at 7200 s on `highp`). The home shader reads cover, overcast and high cirrus from `u_weather` and nothing else: the rain, snow, fog, storm flash and snowy ground code (`u_fall`, `u_ground`) is gone from it, as are `cloudGlsl` and `spaceGlsl`. The Earth is never still (owner call 2026-10-14: animated, and the mouse moves it): `orbitStep` (`sky-scene.ts`, tested) slides the ground and both cloud layers past the eye at 2.4 km/s (`orbitSpeed`; 0.9 sideways and 0.45 toward the viewer in the camera frame, so the flow looks the same at any aim), summed in `u_orbit` (metres, wrapped at 100,000 km so a float32 keeps 8 m of precision; off on a device without `highp` in fragment shaders). It only runs while the sky runs, so reduced motion, the pause button and a still frame keep the Earth still. Shooting stars (`u_meteor` and `u_meteor2`, two slots, screen space, each launched by `launch()` every 2.2 to 8 s with a 1.45 s flight) are drawn by `shootingStar` in the home shader only: a bright head with a halo, a tapering trail 0.6 view long that cools from white-blue to amber, a little sparkle along it, over the black sky above the limb (`x (1 - cover)`, 0.8x by day). `meteorTail` and `meteorWidth` stay for the blog shader through `skyCommon`, so any home look change goes in the home block only. A fast pointer (`gust.energy` above 0.7, at most one per 2.6 s) fires an idle slot at once. Pointer: on fine pointers `lookTarget` turns the camera up to 2 degrees of yaw, 1.6 of pitch and 4.5 of roll toward the pointer (eased at rate 4); the roll tilts the whole Earth around the line of sight (`cameraBasis(yaw, pitch, roll)`, the side under the pointer dips, the limb swings about 55 px at each edge on a laptop), and the stars, sun and moon turn with it. Moving the pointer anywhere in the window (`stirClouds`, the whole screen in home mode) also feeds `gust.energy`, and `orbitBoost` turns that into up to 8x orbit speed that fades in about 1.5 s, so the clouds and the land rush by while you move. Reduced motion, pause and a stopped sky keep it still. A swipe puff sits on the cloud deck where the pointer ray meets it (`worldPuff(ray, altitude, wind)` returns nothing for a ray that hits no deck, so a swipe over the sky adds no puff; at most 6, rise 0.7 s to 0.9, settle over 4 s). Text over the sky (owner calls 2026-10-13: no boxes, no shadows): six `text-sky` texts with `inkTargets` in `src/lib/sky-ink.ts` (name 3, bio 4.5, copyright 4.5), `text-sky` is `color-mix(in srgb, var(--ink), white calc(var(--lift) * 100%))`, and `createSkyProbe` (`src/lib/sky-probe.ts`) draws the sky shader into a 24x6 sample grid per text box through `u_probe`, reads it back one frame later, and `skyLevel`, `liftFor` and `nextLift` pick the lift (98th percentile plus 0.02, steps of 0.05, target x 1.35, raise at once, lower past 0.12). The probe does not see the star points. Glare and moon halo under the text are capped at 0.3 (`glareText`, `textCap`, `u_txt`). There is no weather data, no `/weather` fetch and no `?weather=`: `?at=` pins the time and also stops the page from counting as a crawler, which keeps test runs on the live sky. Frame pacing is unchanged: 0.75x (then 0.5x, then 0.35x without clouds), at most 1.4 M pixels, about 90 fps, 30 fps after 800 ms idle, pause when hidden or blurred, freeze after eight frames over 100 ms, one still frame under reduced motion and on a software renderer (`src/lib/webgl.ts`), `createFrameClock` for `u_time`.
- Text limit: behind the same three text boxes (64 px feather at 900 px tall) `capText` in the shared `textShade` GLSL rolls the sky luminance off above 0.10 toward 0.16 (linear) in every phase, just under the global roll-off (0.17, `homeCeiling`), weighted by `textNear` (260 px around the text), so no box shows on a bright day sky; with the text lifted to white that still gives 4.5:1. It only darkens bright spots such as a lit cloud or the sun glare near the name; a dark sky is untouched. The contrast audit found 70 readings under target without it (2026-10-13).
- No renderer blocks on shader compiles: the star points (`star-gl.ts`) poll `COMPLETION_STATUS_KHR` on a 16 ms timer and look up their attributes only once linked (an early `getAttribLocation` blocked the page 80 to 250 ms on a cold compile, measured 2026-10-14); the planets link their programs with `KHR_parallel_shader_compile` and poll before the first draw (`whenLinked` in `planet-gl.ts`; the context options, the software-renderer check, the clear state, the canvas size, the frame loop and the noise GLSL are shared by the repo planets through `planet-stage.ts` and `planet-gl.ts`, link errors checked only outside production); the globe, the only three.js user (a lazy chunk loaded on its click), compiles with `renderer.compileAsync` and sets `renderer.debug.checkShaderErrors` only outside production. The planet port (2026-10-12) kept every GLSL body, the geometry, the sRGB-to-linear uniform colors, the draw states and order, and took about 104 KB brotli off the first visit (0 pixel diff on the still frame). Measured with `~/powertools/scripts/site-probe.mjs`: no blocking GL calls, startup long tasks fell from 955 ms to 67 ms.
- Sky modes (`SpaceBackground` `mode`, `src/lib/sky-preset.ts`): `home` is the 100 km sky above. `blog` is one sky for every blog page (owner calls 2026-10-13, Q7 and Q17): mounted once by `src/layouts/BlogLayout.astro` inside `[data-blog]`, a fixed `div[data-blog-sky]` (one screen plus `--sky-travel` 40vh) with the canvas inside, the clear night (`blogLook()`) with the LinkedIn cover rays (owner pick A3, 2026-10-13: `longRaysShader` from `src/lib/sky-rays.ts`, `coronaSize` 1.6 and `coronaBright` 1.3, made by `patchShader`, the same copy `scripts/generate-images.ts` draws the cover with; the home shader is untouched), the corona source off canvas at the top-left (`cornerCorona(view)`, r = 0.43 x the view, where `u_view` is 72% of the screen height, at most 960 CSS px, so the look matches the old 72svh post header), and a shooting star on `cornerMeteor` (y measured from the top of the view, x pulled left on narrow screens), 1.6 s every 6 s, only while the sky runs and never for crawlers. No weather fetch, no clock refresh, no corona layer, no `sky:preset` event, no per-post sky. Parallax runs on the compositor (`sky-drift`, a scroll-driven `translate` of the wrapper to -40vh over the first 100vh; one `requestAnimationFrame` per scroll where `animation-timeline` is missing), never as a shader redraw per scroll frame. A scroll-driven `[data-sky-fade]` layer fades the whole sky to 80% `--background` over the header height and `::after` darkens its lower part, so body text keeps at least 9.9:1. It runs while `[data-sky-sentinel]` (the first 50vh of the page) is in view and holds a still frame below; reduced motion, the pause button, a software renderer, Save-Data and crawlers get a still sky and no parallax (`data-sky-still` for crawlers and Save-Data). Ink targets: `postInkTargets` (equal to `blogIndexInkTargets`), the title and meta line in `[data-sky-header]`. The full description is in the **Blog** bullets "Sky modes" and "Blog chrome" of `docs/architecture.md`.
